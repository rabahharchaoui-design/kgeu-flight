## SESSION 3: FLIGHT FEEL AND VISUALS
Tag: pre-feel. Branch: feel. Report: FEEL_REPORT.md.

- [ ] 3.0 CONTROLS FIRST: MULTI TOUCH AND NO HIDDEN MENU. (a) While the thumb holds the stick, nothing else can be tapped (gear, flaps, the More button). Every on screen control must work at the same time as the others. Use Pointer Events and track each touch by pointerId: each control owns only the pointer that started on it, touch-action: none on controls, and no document wide touch handler, global preventDefault, or pointer capture that blocks other elements. Test with real multi touch in Playwright (CDP Input.dispatchTouchEvent with two touch points): hold the stick and tap gear, flaps up, flaps down, brake, and camera, confirm each works while the stick keeps steering. (b) Remove the "More" button. Put every needed control on screen, chosen by skill mode and aircraft, without crowding. Rookie: stick, throttle, camera, map, pause (gear and flaps are automatic). Pilot: add GEAR, FLAPS UP, FLAPS DOWN with a small current flap indicator, and BRAKE (hold). Per aircraft: no GEAR button on fixed gear aircraft (Cessna 172, Pipistrel Alpha); no flaps buttons where flaps are automatic (F-16); drones get a SENSOR button; the C-130 gets DROP during airdrop missions. Group buttons in a tidy column near the right thumb above the throttle: 44pt minimum, icon plus short text label, semi transparent. Rare things (photo mode, radio, settings, restart) live in the labeled pause menu. Every button added later in this session must follow the same multi touch rules and layout.
- [ ] 3.1 F-16 ROLL SMOOTHING. In Pilot mode, rolls, barrel rolls, and inverted flight jerk and stutter. Check first: Euler angles or gimbal lock in attitude integration, or the chase camera using a fixed world up vector that flips when inverted. Use quaternions for attitude and camera, let the chase camera's up follow the aircraft's up smoothly, check for dt spikes. Add a Playwright test flying scripted 360 degree rolls, a barrel roll, and a loop, logging per frame attitude and camera change, failing on any discontinuity. Apply to every aircraft.
- [ ] 3.2 NIGHT LIGHTING FIX. (a) Aircraft lights: web search real light placement per type (Cessna 172, Pipistrel Alpha, F-16, MQ-9A, MQ-9B, C-130H) and place them accurately. Baseline FAA rules: steady red left wingtip, steady green right wingtip, steady white aft on the tail; red and green visible from straight ahead to 110 degrees to their side; white covers 70 degrees either side of straight back. Anticollision: white wingtip strobes with a quick double flash and a red flashing beacon (fin or fuselage top, plus belly on the C-130), 40 to 100 flashes per minute. Aircraft specific details like the F-16 formation strip lights. Landing and taxi lights in real positions, on automatically below 1,000 ft AGL and on the ground, with a realistic light pool on the runway. (b) Runway and ground lights: replace dome glows with small sharp points, small at distance, only slightly bigger up close, a tiny tight halo at most, no hemispheres or blobs. White edge lights (yellow on the last 2,000 ft), green threshold, red runway end, blue taxiway edges. Match real night photos of Luke AFB and Glendale (web search). Build as a reusable airport lighting system for more airports. (c) Night clouds dark and subtle, faintly moonlit on top, never flat gray. (d) Very subtle bloom, nothing smeared. (e) Screenshots: night approaches to Luke and Glendale before and after, plus each aircraft at night from the side and from behind, into overnight-screenshots/night.
- [ ] 3.3 CAMERA VIEWS, DRONE SENSOR BALL, DROP CAMERA. (a) Remove tower view and crowd view from the View button for every aircraft; keep the rest including the drone nose camera. (b) MQ-9A and MQ-9B only: sensor ball view from the turret under the nose. DAY TV: color, slightly desaturated, light grain, subtle compression feel. IR: grayscale thermal with WHITE HOT and BLACK HOT toggle; hot things stand out (engines, exhausts, vehicles, runway lights, city buildings), light sensor noise. (c) iPhone controls: drag to slew, pinch or zoom buttons for 3 or 4 zoom steps, a mode button cycling DAY TV / IR WHITE HOT / IR BLACK HOT, a LOCK button holding the ball on the ground point in the crosshair. (d) Overlay: simple generic game style with crosshair, zoom, mode, ball azimuth and elevation, altitude, heading, and an AUTOPILOT tag. Original; do not copy any real ground control station display. (e) AUTOPILOT: entering the sensor view engages autopilot with a brief "AUTOPILOT ENGAGED"; holds altitude and flies a smooth orbit around the point the ball looks at (or the locked point); if not looking at the ground, holds wings level, altitude, heading. After leaving the sensor view it stays on until the thumb stick moves, then disconnects with a short tone and "AUTOPILOT OFF". (f) Rookie labels: "Day camera" and "Heat camera". (g) Screenshots of DAY TV, IR white hot, IR black hot looking at Luke from about 15,000 ft into overnight-screenshots/sensor. (h) C-130 DROP CAMERA: during airdrop and care package missions, a free look pad in the upper right: drag to swing the camera around the aircraft including straight back and down at the drop zone, release to snap back. A "Look back" button jumps to a view from the open cargo ramp toward the drop zone. After release, the camera follows the pallet down under its parachute until it lands, shows distance from center, then returns to the aircraft. Never overlaps the throttle or other buttons.
- [ ] 3.4 STRIKE MISSION: EASIER TARGETS, BIGGER EXPLOSIONS. (a) Each target gets a clear diamond marker in the sensor view with distance, off screen arrows point to targets outside the view, a NEXT TARGET button slews the ball to the next one, targets glow hot in IR, targets are bigger and parked in the open, Rookie NEXT TARGET also auto locks. (b) Explosions much bigger and cinematic: bright fireball, rising smoke column rolling into a mushroom cap, expanding ground shockwave ring, flying debris, secondary explosions on some targets, smoke lingers and drifts with wind, at night the fireball lights the ground, visible in DAY TV and as a huge white hot bloom in IR. Pooled particles, iPhone friendly.
- [ ] 3.5 PROPELLERS. All prop aircraft: individual blades visible at idle and low RPM, then a translucent blurred disc at high RPM with faint blade traces. MQ-9A and MQ-9B: black blades with a yellow stripe across the middle of each blade so the stripes form a faint yellow ring at speed. C-130: 4 blade props on each engine. Cessna and Pipistrel: web search their real prop paint and match it.
- [ ] 3.6 F-16 AFTERBURNER. Nozzle opens as throttle enters afterburner. Flame orange yellow outside, blue white core, visible shock diamonds; length and brightness scale with throttle, subtle flicker. At night much bigger and brighter, lighting the tail and the ground below on takeoff. Deep afterburner rumble and crackle in the engine sound. At military power only a faint heat haze.
- [ ] 3.7 HABOOB DUST STORM. The game's signature weather moment. Add "Haboob" as a fourth option next to Day, Sunset, and Night on the fly screen and in the pause menu Change flight panel. A towering wall of orange brown dust rolls across the desert; visibility drops as it arrives, the sun becomes a dim disc, gusty turbulence and crosswind hit, ATC calls out reduced visibility. Flying into the wall looks dramatic from outside. Smooth on iPhone.
- [ ] 3.8 CALLSIGN CHECK. The radio callsign must match the selected aircraft at every spawn point (a Viper callsign was heard on an MQ-9 at Luke). Fix any mismatch.
- [ ] 3.9 CRASH EXPLOSIONS. Every crash explodes, scaled by aircraft, using the strike explosion system. Pipistrel and Cessna: medium fireball, burning wreck, black smoke column. MQ-9A and MQ-9B: medium fireball, scattered debris, wings breaking off. F-16: big fast fuel fireball, long debris trail along the impact path, burning wreck. C-130: the biggest explosion in the game, a huge multi stage fireball as fuel tanks go up in sequence, a massive rolling mushroom of black smoke, fuselage breaking into pieces, wide debris field, secondary explosions, a wreck that burns for a long time. All crashes: camera shake, a deep boom arriving a beat after the flash at distance, at night the fire lights the ground, smoke visible from miles away. Then a short "Crashed" card that doesn't block the view for 3 seconds, with RETRY and MENU buttons. Pooled particles.
- [ ] 3.10 @OHRABAH EASTER EGGS. Tasteful "@OhRabah" placements with YouTube's official red play button icon unmodified: a hangar banner at Glendale, a lit billboard near State Farm Stadium, a rooftop sign in downtown Phoenix, a small flag at the C-130 drop zone, and a Cessna banner tow plane slowly circling the Glendale area towing an "@OhRabah" banner. Billboards lit at night, text readable up close. Civilian spots only: nothing on Luke AFB, nothing painted on the F-16, MQ-9s, or C-130.

## SESSION 4: WORLD AIRPORTS AND TRAFFIC
Tag: pre-world. Branch: world. Report: WORLD_REPORT.md. One commit for the traffic system, then one per airport.

- [ ] 4.1 AIR TRAFFIC AND TCAS (shared system, built first). AI traffic: 6 to 10 aircraft at a time around the player, simple low poly models in plain generic liveries with no real airline names or logos. Arizona: airliners arriving and departing Sky Harbor, Cessnas flying left traffic at Glendale, F-16s flying the overhead pattern at Luke (initial, pitch out, closed pattern), a helicopter or two over Phoenix. Traffic lands, rolls out, taxis off, takes off, and never drives through the player on the ground. Nav lights and strobes at night. ATC traffic advisories on the radio ("Traffic, two o'clock, three miles, Cessna, same altitude") via the existing say pipeline. TCAS modeled on real TCAS II: traffic on the mini map and a small semi transparent HUD overlay with standard symbols: hollow white diamond (other traffic), filled white diamond (proximate, within about 6 nm and 1,200 ft), filled amber circle (Traffic Advisory), filled red square (Resolution Advisory), each with relative altitude in hundreds of feet (+05, -12) and an up or down arrow if climbing or descending. Traffic Advisory about 40 seconds before closest approach: voice "Traffic, traffic". Resolution Advisory about 25 seconds before: "Climb, climb" or "Descend, descend", with a green band on the vertical speed display for where to fly and red where not to, then "Clear of conflict" once safe; the AI aircraft maneuvers the opposite way. Voice: a calm male cockpit voice from the say pipeline, not on the radio filter. Rookie: plain English ("Plane nearby! Climb now") with a big arrow. Pilot: real callouts only. Inhibit alerts on the ground and below 1,000 ft AGL on final to the player's runway. Traffic is off during missions and arcade games. Low poly with LOD, no traffic simulated outside a 20 nm bubble.
- [ ] 4.2 TOKYO HANEDA (RJTT). Runways built out into Tokyo Bay, water on several sides. Landmarks: Tokyo Tower (red and white), Tokyo Skytree, Rainbow Bridge, a dense skyline, Mt Fuji on the horizon. Night: bright neon city glow and lit towers. Airliner traffic on real active runway directions.
- [ ] 4.3 PARIS CHARLES DE GAULLE (LFPG). The 4 parallel runways and curved terminals. Landmarks: Eiffel Tower (lit gold at night, sparkling for 5 minutes at the top of every hour), Arc de Triomphe, Sacre Coeur on its hill, the Seine, La Defense towers. Airliner traffic on real active runway directions.
- [ ] 4.4 RIO SANTOS DUMONT (SBRJ). Short runway on Guanabara Bay with Sugarloaf right off the end. Landmarks: Sugarloaf with cable car line, Christ the Redeemer on Corcovado (lit at night), Copacabana beach, the bay, green mountains. A perfect C-130 short field challenge. Airliner traffic on real active runway directions.
- [ ] 4.5 DATA AND QUALITY RULES FOR ALL THREE AIRPORTS. Runways from OurAirports (public domain CSV): exact coordinates, headings, lengths, widths, elevations. Taxiways, ramps, terminals, hangars, tower from OpenStreetMap via the Overpass API at build time only, saved as small preprocessed JSON in the repo; never call Overpass from the live game. Terrain from free public elevation data (SRTM or Copernicus DEM), downsampled per region, water where the bay or sea is. Add "Map data © OpenStreetMap contributors" and an OurAirports credit to the Settings credits page. Landmarks need not be to scale; recognizable shapes first, moved closer than real life if needed to be seen on approach and departure, noted in the report. Must look good: runway numbers, centerline, touchdown zone and threshold markings, edge and approach lights using the airport lighting system; taxiway yellow centerlines, hold short markings, yellow and black signs, blue edge lights; terminals with gates and jet bridges, a control tower, hangars, parked generic white airliners and ground vehicles, apron lighting at night; landmarks and skyline framed in view on final and right after takeoff; everything farther away simple.

Fly screen and pause menu Change flight panel: the location row becomes a picker grouped by region: ARIZONA (Glendale, Luke AFB), JAPAN (Tokyo Haneda), FRANCE (Paris CDG), BRAZIL (Rio Santos Dumont), each a small card with a one line description. Aggressor F-16s in blue gray splinter camo (recolor the existing F-16). Rounds of 90 seconds with waves of 1 to 4 bandits. Reuse the afterburner, explosion, and crash systems.
- [ ] 5.2 OFFENSE. Target box on every bandit, edge arrows for off screen bandits. Heat missile (Fox 2): keep a bandit inside the seeker circle, low growl while searching, high steady tone on lock after about 1.5 seconds, FOX 2 button glows when locked; missiles have real flight time, smoke trails, and miss if the bandit breaks hard or flares. Gun: hold GUN, tracers, a lead computing pipper showing where to aim ahead, heavy burst sound, close range only. - [ ] 5.3 DEFENSE. Radar warning: soft ping when a bandit searches for you, fast beeps when locked with amber pulsing screen edges. Missile launch: continuous warning tone, red pulsing edges, cockpit voice "Missile launch", a red arrow with direction and distance. FLARES button with a limited count (about 30): timed right, the missile goes for the flares; a hard break can also beat it. Getting hit: screen shake, smoke, damage; three hits and you eject, round over. Hard deck at 5,000 ft with a "Pull up" warning; below it counts as a training kill against you.

Fun, not brutally hard.
